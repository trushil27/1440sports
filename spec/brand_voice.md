# 1440Sports Brand Voice

How 1440 communicates. Apply when writing any user-facing text — briefs, outreach drafts, digest summaries, MD-bound communications.

1440Sports is a London-based B2B motorsport sponsorship consultancy. Their tagline references "1440 minutes in a day" — the value of every moment of brand exposure during a race weekend.

## Tonal Calibration

### Primary register: Confident, observational, intellectually serious

Not breathless. Not a sales deck. The voice reads like a strategic advisor who has done the work and is sharing what they've found. The reader should feel respected, not pitched.

### Secondary register: Editorial economy

Sentences should feel earned. Avoid corporate filler ("Going forward", "At the end of the day"). Avoid agency clichés ("activate", "amplify", "drive engagement", "leverage", "synergy"). When you need to talk about activation, use the actual specific verb (build, deploy, integrate, demonstrate).

### What to avoid entirely

- Exclamation marks
- "Exciting" / "thrilled" / "pleased to announce"
- "Best-in-class", "world-class", "industry-leading"
- "Proven track record"
- "Innovative solutions"
- Generic inspirational language
- Em dashes used as filler *(only use em dashes when they materially clarify a clause)*

## Specific Phrases That Work

### Strategic framing

- "[X] is the only sponsorship asset that delivers..."
- "The strategic alignment is rare enough to be commercially decisive."
- "The conversation is not [generic version] — it is [specific reframe]."
- "Greenfield" *(for unsaturated categories)*
- "Pre-qualified buyer" *(for Track 2 alumni)*
- "First-100-days window"
- "Brand-reckoning moment" *(for AI unicorn category inflection)*
- "The window closes when [specific timing]"

### Avoid these and rewrite

- ❌ "We can help you reach a global audience" → ✅ "Race-weekend hospitality reaches enterprise CIOs at scale"
- ❌ "F1 is a great platform for B2B" → ✅ "F1's hospitality programmes reach the CTO audience this product targets"
- ❌ "Let's connect for a quick chat" → ✅ "25 minutes when your first-100-days clears"
- ❌ "I think this could be a great opportunity for you" → ✅ "[Specific commercial logic]. Worth 25 minutes?"

## Outreach Email Structure

Default opening hook structure:

```
Hi [First Name],

[Personal reference: shared work, recent news, mutual acquaintance — 1 sentence]

[Specific observation about their situation: what's happening at 
their company that makes this conversation timely — 2 sentences]

[Specific value: what 1440 specifically offers, not generic agency 
language — 1-2 sentences]

[Specific time ask: "25 minutes when [specific time-cue]?"]

[Sign-off]
```

### Example openings (from briefs we've built)

**Mistral AI:**
> *"Following the Accenture partnership and Forge launch — the distribution is set, the technology is set, but the cultural amplifier for enterprise buyers in regulated European industries isn't yet defined. Wanted to share why Alpine specifically is the sharpest tool for that job. 25 minutes?"*

**Factory AI:**
> *"Watching the Series C and the London office news. The UK enterprise audience you need to convert is exactly where Williams Racing's hospitality reaches — and they're twenty miles from your new office. Wanted to share why this is the sharpest tool for your first ninety days. 25 minutes?"*

**Luminary Cloud (Track 2 alumni):**
> *"Pete, congrats on the Series B and the Northrop Grumman work. We worked alongside you on the Workday-McLaren activation in 2023 through 1440Sports. Watching Luminary now — the F1 narrative is more direct than anything Workday could claim. Wanted to share three deal architectures that fit Luminary's stage. 25 minutes when you're back from GTC?"*

## When Drafting for the MD *(internal communications)*

The MD *(Ricky Paugh)* values:
- Compound evidence ("not just one, but here are three")
- Score transparency (he sees through padded analysis)
- Direct recommendation, not options paralysis
- Time-bound action items

Default structure for MD-bound briefs:

```
[Headline framing — what does this surface?]

[Three concrete signals or one developed signal — depending on 
context]

[Single recommendation with timing]

[Honest risk acknowledgement]

[Specific next step]
```

## When Talking About 1440 to Outsiders *(in briefs/emails)*

- "1440Sports" *(no space, capital S)* — internal name
- "1440 Sports" with space — acceptable in formal copy
- Reference London office naturally
- Reference past client work *(Udemy/McLaren, Workday/McLaren)* when relevant — this is reputation capital
- Avoid bragging language; let the work speak

## When Talking About Targets

- "Decision-maker" not "decision-makers" — be specific about one person
- "C-suite" only when verifiable, not as filler
- Names always include verification path *(LinkedIn confirmed, press release confirmed)*
- Roles always exact *(don't say "marketing leader" when you mean "CMO")*

## When Talking About Competitors / Other Agencies

Don't. The brief never mentions other sponsorship agencies, even to dismiss them. Position 1440 by what it delivers, not by contrast.

Exception: when discussing saturation, it's fine to acknowledge "every sports agency targets [X] this quarter" — that's an accurate market observation, not a positioning statement.
