Why Cadillac F1 Team — the open lane, the audience, the platform

The open lane
Cadillac's 2026 roster is entirely inaugural: TWG AI, Claro, Core Scientific, IFS, Jim Beam, Tenneco, Tommy Hilfiger, Pirelli, Alpinestars and TWG Motorsports, with General Motors as parent. IFS runs operations software, Core Scientific the data centre and TWG AI the models. There is no cybersecurity partner, and the team profile lists cybersecurity as an open category.
A team standing up its security from nothing
Cadillac is designing and building its cars at Silverstone while its Fishers, Indiana campus is under construction, with US bases in Charlotte and Warren. A team building its IT estate across two countries in year one is choosing its security operations model now, and a managed 24/7 SOC is the product built for a team that has no security team of its own yet.
An American story for an American team
TENEX.AI is headquartered in Sarasota, Florida, funded in part by the Florida Opportunity Fund, and sells to Fortune 500 and Global 2000 buyers. Cadillac is the American flag-bearer on the grid; a US security unicorn protecting the US debut team is a story both sides can sell at home.
Why not the other open seats
Haas and Racing Bulls also carry no cybersecurity partner. Haas has Mphasis providing data, analytics and security services, which crowds the lane, and Racing Bulls' roster is built for a younger consumer audience. Cadillac offers the cleanest category, the strongest US-market fit and the greenfield workstream.

A race team's most valuable assets are data: car design files, race strategy, real-time telemetry between the track and the factory, and supplier links into GM. TENEX.AI would run the team's security operations centre: AI agents triaging and investigating every alert in under a minute, human analysts governing every response, continuous cover across US and UK time zones, and an incident-response retainer for race weekends.
