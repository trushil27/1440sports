The Case

The company was founded in January 2026 out of Toyota Research Institute by Russ Tedrake, the MIT professor who ran TRI's large-behavior-model work. Its robots have two arms and a sensor head on a wheeled base, no legs, and run on Large Behavior Models that learn a task through practice. At a Toyota plant in North America they went from first pilot to production in under two months and now run shifts alongside human teams: machine tending, tool setting, parts kitting, loading and unloading. Strategic partners span automotive, aerospace, semiconductors, electronics and logistics.

No robotics brand sits on the 2026 F1 grid, and the one team titled by Walden's lead investor runs its own CNC shop: Haas is owned by Gene Haas, founder of Haas Automation, the largest CNC machine-tool builder in North America. Machine tending is the task Walden's robots already do at Toyota. The lane is open and the workstream is literal.

Why Now

The seed closed on 15 July and the deployments it funds are the story; a company hiring against a $300M cheque needs a stage engineers watch. Teams close 2027 rosters in the fourth quarter, Austin and Las Vegas sit inside the window, and the winter build is when a factory pilot runs.

WHY TGR HAAS F1 TEAM

Toyota Gazoo Racing took the title at Haas from 2026 after a 2024 technical partnership, and Toyota co-led Walden's seed: the introduction runs through the investor. Haas is the American privateer, based in Kannapolis and Banbury, with a CNC shop at the centre of its operations. Formula E is closed to the category: ABB, a robotics and automation group, has been its title partner since 2018. Carmaker-aligned teams are awkward for a Toyota-backed company; Williams is the only other independent.

VALUE TO TGR HAAS F1 TEAM  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A. Walden robots on the Kannapolis and Banbury floors doing what they do at Toyota: tending the team's CNC machines, tool setting, kitting parts for the car build and loading between stations, on a pilot-to-production cycle Toyota ran in under two months. In return: Official Robotics Partner designation, sidepod and garage presence, Austin and Las Vegas hospitality for the manufacturers Walden sells to, and a content series.
