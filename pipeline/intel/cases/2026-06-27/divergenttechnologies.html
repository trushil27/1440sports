The Case

On 17 June Divergent announced Monolith One, a laser powder-bed printer with twelve 2kW lasers, and a second factory: 430,000 sq ft in Long Beach, California, 64 printers, production from early 2027, an 8X rise in annual output. It follows the $290M Series E closed on 15 September 2025 at a $2.3B valuation, led by Rochefort Asset Management, after a $230M Series D in November 2023. The platform, DAPS, pairs AI-enabled design with metal additive and robotic assembly, and has delivered 600+ unique parts for Lockheed Martin, RTX, McLaren, Aston Martin and Bugatti.

Divergent already builds the McLaren W1's Formula 1-inspired front suspension: titanium upper and lower wishbones and the upright, printed by Divergent under a multi-year collaboration with McLaren Automotive. The road-car side of the brand is a customer; the racing side, operationally separate under Zak Brown, is not yet a partner. A second factory needs a flagship to fill it, and a race car is the fastest proof a structural printer can give.

Why Now

The factory and the printer were announced on 17 June, with a new COO, CTO and Chief Commercial Officer named in the same month: a company staffing up to sell capacity. Silverstone falls days after the row; Monza and Austin sit inside the window, and 2027 rosters close in the fourth quarter.

WHY MCLAREN MASTERCARD F1 TEAM

McLaren's roster is engineering-led, Dell, Cisco, Google Cloud, Groq, Arrow Electronics, but carries no additive or digital-manufacturing partner; Stratasys supplies polymer printing, a different lane from metal structures. The warm path is real: McLaren Automotive's engineers iterated the W1 wishbones on DAPS, and CYVN, which owns Automotive, holds a non-controlling stake in Racing. Alpine has 3D Systems and Haas is owned by a machine-tool brand; McLaren is where the printed-suspension story lives.

VALUE TO MCLAREN MASTERCARD F1 TEAM  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A - operational. Titanium uprights, wishbone prototypes, brackets and cooling manifolds designed with DAPS' AI-enabled optimisation and printed on Monolith One, with robotic assembly for sub-structures: the workflow that produced the W1's suspension, applied to a race car's fast-iteration parts inside the cost cap. McLaren gets shorter design-to-track cycles and a US manufacturing source; Divergent gets a flagship for the new factory.

Deal Architecture

Entry as Official Digital Manufacturing Partner. THREE YEARS, 2027-2029, spanning the Long Beach ramp. Estimated $3-5M a year, part-payable in printed parts and engineering time. Scope: sidepod placement, naming of a printed-structures programme, MTC content, hospitality at Silverstone, Austin and Las Vegas.

Risks & Counters

DEFENCE IDENTITY  The June story is Tomahawk airframes; a weapons-manufacturing partner may not suit McLaren's consumer-facing brand mix. Counter: Build the programme on the automotive lineage, W1, Bugatti, Aston Martin, and keep the activation technical.
