The Case

Zenobē announced on 15 June that 22 banks, NatWest, Lloyds, Santander and Aviva among them, had committed c.£980M to its UK and Ireland electric-fleet facility: £480M of long-dated term debt, a £400M capex line and £100M of ancillaries. Capital raised since 2017 now exceeds £3.2B. Nine days later it closed a $400M platform for zero-emission fleets in Australia and New Zealand, and on 27 July it bought Bavaria's sdp energie with a 1.75 GW German battery pipeline. KKR and Infracapital have been joint majority owners since a c.£870M equity round in September 2023.

This is a company that has already paid for motorsport in kind: its second-life bus-battery powerskids were Extreme E's official energy-storage supply from the series' first season to its final event in October 2025. With Extreme E gone, that budget line is orphaned. Zenobē now sells fleet electrification to bus operators in Britain, Europe, North America and Australasia, and the buyers it needs are transport authorities, fleet owners and the banks that fund them.

Why Now

Three financings in six weeks reset the marketing calendar. Formula E's GEN4 era opens in Jeddah on 18 December, and the London E-Prix moves to Brands Hatch on 29-30 May 2027: the reigning champions' home race, forty miles from Zenobē's London office, is the natural launch stage.

WHY JAGUAR TCS RACING

Jaguar TCS Racing are the reigning Teams' World Champions, run from Kidlington, Oxfordshire, and committed to GEN4 through 2030. The roster in the sponsor table is TCS as title partner and Castrol; there is no fleet, charging or storage brand, so the lane is open. A British electrification operator on the British champions' car at a British home race is the cleanest story on the grid.

VALUE TO JAGUAR TCS RACING  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A - operational. Zenobē's second-life battery powerskids powered five Extreme E paddocks; at Jaguar they can carry garage and hospitality load at European rounds where event-power rules allow, cutting generator diesel on camera. Off track, Zenobē electrifies the team's Kidlington depot and support fleet under its fleet-as-a-service model. Commercially: Brands Hatch hospitality for transport authorities, fleet owners and lenders, and an Official Fleet Electrification Partner designation.

Deal Architecture

Entry as Official Fleet Electrification and Energy Storage Partner. THREE YEARS, Seasons 13-15 (2026/27 to 2028/29), from the GEN4 debut through the first two Brands Hatch home races. Estimated £1-2M a year, part-payable in powerskid supply and depot electrification. Scope: sidepod branding, Brands Hatch and Berlin hospitality, paddock-power content, and a bus-operator guest programme.

Risks & Counters

DEBT, NOT EQUITY  The £980M is bank debt for buses and chargers, not a marketing budget, and the company's revenue is not public. Counter: Price the entry at £1-2M with in-kind supply; the Extreme E precedent shows the budget line exists.
CHAMPIONSHIP RULES  Event power at an E-Prix is the championship's remit; team-level powerskid use needs Formula E's approval. Counter: Start with garage, hospitality and depot load, which need no approval; add trackside supply later.
