The Case

Fairmat (Paris, founded 2020) uses a patented cold-atmospheric-plasma process to recycle aerospace and wind carbon-fibre waste into FairPly, a new structural composite - closing the loop on a material that is otherwise landfilled or burned. It raised a EUR51.5M Series B in Apr 2025 (EUR26.5M equity + EUR25M EIB venture debt; Bpifrance and Slate VC leading, with Temasek, Pictet, Singular, CNP and Infinity Recycling), ~$114M in total, and already sells to Hexcel, Siemens Gamesa and sports brands (Decathlon, DPS Skis). It has never touched motorsport. The fit is with F1, not FE, for a concrete reason: FE uses a spec chassis (Spark builds the common monocoque for all teams), so recycled composite there is a championship-level choice - whereas an F1 constructor designs and manufactures its OWN carbon-fibre car (~85% of it composite), so Fairmat is a genuine team-level materials workstream and a direct lever on F1's Net Zero 2030 pledge. HONEST framing: near-term use is non-structural/bodywork, garage and facility composites plus verified sustainability data, co-developing toward structural parts (homologation makes fully-structural longer-dated) - so it is a WARM, real-but-scaling MODE-A. Decision-maker: Founder & CEO Benjamin Saada.

Why Now

F1's Net Zero 2030 commitment and the cost cap are pushing teams toward sustainable, lower-cost materials now, and Fairmat is scaling post-Series-B with FairPly in commercial demand - so a founding materials relationship is best built early, before a rival team or composite brand claims the 'recycled carbon fibre' position. The honest why-now is positional, not a dated trigger.

Why Williams

Williams is an engineering-revival constructor that designs and builds its own carbon-fibre car, with a materials/sustainability lane that is open (its Atlassian, VAST, Keeper and Anthropic deals are all non-materials), so a recycled-composite partner is a genuine engineering and Net-Zero story rather than a logo - and a right-sized, mutual innovation partnership for a midfield team using material smarts to punch up. No partner conflict (Fairmat's backers are diversified funds, none a Williams rival).

Value to Williams  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

A TWO-WAY exchange (tech + business both directions), not sponsorship cash - three concrete swaps. (1) MATERIAL <-> PROVING GROUND: Fairmat supplies FairPly recycled carbon fibre for the team's composite parts (bodywork, garage/transport and facility structures, co-developing toward more), cutting material cost and footprint; in return the F1 car - the most extreme structural test on earth - becomes Fairmat's ultimate performance validation and global reference, de-risking FairPly for its aerospace/auto buyers. (2) SUSTAINABILITY DATA <-> CREDIBILITY: Fairmat gives the team auditable recycled-content and CO2-avoided data that feeds its FIA / F1 Net-Zero-2030 reporting and a real (non-greenwash) circular-materials story; the team gives Fairmat a global sustainability-leadership stage and a marquee engineering brand association. (3) CO-ENGINEERING R&D <-> BUYER NETWORK: joint R&D subjecting recycled composite to F1 loads advances FairPly's spec and IP; the team's paddock and its OEM/aerospace ecosystem open exactly the industrial composite buyers (the Hexcel/Siemens-Gamesa world) Fairmat needs to scale. Williams gets cost-down sustainable material + a hard ESG story; Fairmat gets validation, a stage and buyers.

Deal Architecture

Official Sustainable-Materials / Recycled-Composite Partner on Williams, minimum three years, ~$2-4M/yr (right-sized to a scale-up), structured as a co-development + material-supply workstream (non-structural and facility composites first, structural R&D roadmap) plus a joint Net-Zero-2030 sustainability-data and content programme - value exchanged both ways, not a pure logo fee.
