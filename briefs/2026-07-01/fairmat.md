## THE CASE
Fairmat (Paris, founded 2020) uses a patented cold-atmospheric-plasma process to recycle aerospace and wind carbon-fibre waste into FairPly, a new structural composite - closing the loop on a material that is otherwise landfilled or burned. It raised a EUR51.5M Series B in Apr 2025 (EUR26.5M equity + EUR25M EIB venture debt; Bpifrance and Slate VC leading, with Temasek, Pictet, Singular, CNP and Infinity Recycling), ~$114M in total, and already sells to Hexcel, Siemens Gamesa and sports brands (Decathlon, DPS Skis). It has never touched motorsport. The fit is with F1, not FE, for a concrete reason: FE uses a spec chassis (Spark builds the common monocoque for all teams), so recycled composite there is a championship-level choice - whereas an F1 constructor designs and manufactures its OWN carbon-fibre car (~85% of it composite), so Fairmat is a genuine team-level materials workstream and a direct lever on F1's Net Zero 2030 pledge. HONEST framing: near-term use is non-structural/bodywork, garage and facility composites plus verified sustainability data, co-developing toward structural parts (homologation makes fully-structural longer-dated) - so it is a WARM, real-but-scaling MODE-A. Decision-maker: Founder & CEO Benjamin Saada.

## WHY NOW
F1's Net Zero 2030 commitment and the cost cap are pushing teams toward sustainable, lower-cost materials now, and Fairmat is scaling post-Series-B with FairPly in commercial demand - so a founding materials relationship is best built early, before a rival team or composite brand claims the 'recycled carbon fibre' position. The honest why-now is positional, not a dated trigger.
