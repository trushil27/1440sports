The Case

Eaton, the intelligent-power-management leader, posted record FY25 revenue of $27.4B (+10%) and is concentrating on its core Electrical and Aerospace businesses as electrification, AI-data-centre power and reindustrialisation surge (it is spinning off its smaller Vehicle/eMobility segment). It has never entered F1. The fit is on-profile (the nVent/Versigent electrical thread, at large-cap scale and public) and real: a modern F1 operation - factory, wind tunnel, driver-in-loop simulator, energy-hungry compute and trackside - runs on resilient electrical power, exactly Eaton's domain. Aston Martin, which built a brand-new campus (factory, wind tunnel, simulator) and now runs its own works power-unit programme, is an intensive electrical-load build with an open electrical lane (its partners are compute/AI/data: Aramco, CoreWeave, Arm, NetApp, Cognition). Verified decision path: CEO Paulo Ruiz (C-level sponsor) and Global Marketing & Communications Director Camie Hanily (brand/sponsorship owner), with Eaton's data-centre/electrical commercial leadership as technical counterpart.

Why Now

Eaton is at the crest of the electrification and AI-power demand cycle, with record revenue and brand investment to match, and F1's 2026 electrical-heavy rules make a power-management partner on-message. Aston Martin's new campus and works power-unit programme mean its electrical infrastructure is scaling now.

Why Aston Martin

Aston Martin's purpose-built campus - factory, wind tunnel, state-of-the-art simulator - plus a new works power-unit programme is one of the most electrically intensive builds on the grid, and its partner roster is compute/AI/data, not electrical-power, so the lane is open. Eaton can underpin the team's power resilience and efficiency as a real workstream.

Value to Aston Martin  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A, real mechanics. (1) TECHNICAL (the core): a championship F1 operation is electrically intensive - a factory, a wind tunnel, a driver-in-loop simulator, energy-hungry CFD/compute and trackside garages that cannot lose power - and Eaton's intelligent power management (UPS, switchgear, distribution, energy optimisation) underpins exactly that, improving resilience and energy efficiency across the campus and at the track: a genuine, deployable workstream, not signage. (2) COMMERCIAL / B2B: Eaton sells to data-centre, industrial and utility buyers who populate the F1 paddock - hospitality becomes a pipeline, while Aston gains a deep-pocketed (~$100B+) electrical anchor. (3) BRAND / TALENT: 'the power behind performance' ties Eaton to F1's electrification era, with an electrical-engineering talent halo. Aston gets power resilience/efficiency; Eaton gets a visible reference at its peak.

Deal Architecture

Official Power-Management / Electrical Partner on Aston Martin, minimum three years, ~$4-6M/yr, with category exclusivity in electrical power management and a deployment across the team's campus and trackside power.
