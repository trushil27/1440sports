## THE CASE
Honeywell is splitting into three public companies, and the final step - spinning off Honeywell Aerospace as a standalone business - distributes on 29 June 2026. The new company will have more than $17B in annual revenue and be one of the largest publicly-traded aerospace suppliers, with leading positions in propulsion, cockpit and navigation systems, and auxiliary power. Two things make it worth a conversation. First, a spin-off is a brand-establishment moment: a newly independent public company must build an identity distinct from its former parent, which is exactly when first-mover brand investments get approved. Second, aerospace precision-engineering carries a genuine F1 halo - propulsion, materials and systems engineering are the sport's own language. The honest counterweight: this is a large, established B2B/B2G supplier whose customers (airlines, OEMs, defence) are won through procurement, not paddock hospitality, so its appetite for a motorsport sponsorship is unproven. Treat this as a relationship-and-halo play to open now, not a deal to force.

## WHY NOW
The 29 June spin-off creates a finite brand-establishment window - new CEO Jim Currier and a fresh standalone leadership team will set the independent company's identity and brand-investment agenda in its first two quarters. Opening a conversation around the listing positions 1440 early, before the new entity's priorities harden, even though this is a longer-horizon relationship than a pre-IPO growth brand.
