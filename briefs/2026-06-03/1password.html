The Case

1Password has raised $920M (last round at a $6.8B valuation) and has crossed ~$400M in ARR (up from $331M in 2024), with more than 180,000 business customers, gross retention above 90% and positive free cash flow. CEO David Faugno has said the company is weighing a 2026 or 2027 IPO - it is 'not rushing' - so this is a genuine pre-listing brand-building window rather than a dated scramble. Enterprise identity and access security is unrepresented in Formula E, and 1Password's North American brand maps cleanly onto Andretti, whose enterprise-friendly, sustainability-forward audience matches its buyer profile. A pre-IPO multi-year partnership lets the company build enterprise credibility while entering a category no rival holds.

Why Now

Pre-IPO brand investment plus an open category in a growing, enterprise-credible championship make this the moment to plant a flag - before the listing (which CEO David Faugno is weighing for 2026/2027), when category exclusivity is cheapest, and while a refreshed leadership team shapes the next-phase brand.

Why Andretti Formula E

Andretti's North American identity and Formula E growth platform fit 1Password's brand and buyer base, and Andretti's recent embrace of technology partners (including an AI partnership) shows an organisation receptive to enterprise-tech storytelling rather than pure consumer signage.

Value to Andretti Formula E  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A. A racing organisation's distributed staff, drivers and partners need secure credential and access management across many systems and locations; 1Password is a real, deployable operational partner. The deal can include securing the team's own access infrastructure, giving Andretti genuine value and 1Password an authentic 'who has access' story plus enterprise hospitality.

Deal Architecture

Official Identity Security Partner tier on Andretti Formula E, minimum three years, ~$3-4M/yr, with category exclusivity and a working deployment for the team.

Melton Littlepage
