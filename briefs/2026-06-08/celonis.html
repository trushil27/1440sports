The Case

Celonis pioneered process mining - using a company's own event-log data to expose and fix operational inefficiency - and has grown into a ~$13B European enterprise leader on roughly $1.77B raised, repeatedly described as IPO-ready. On 12 May 2026 it launched its Context Model and agreed to acquire AI decision-intelligence firm Ikigai Labs - a clear scale-up-and-reposition moment that brings fresh brand attention. Its core promise - doing more with less, cutting waste through efficiency - maps unusually well onto Formula E's enterprise-and-sustainability audience, and as a German champion it pairs naturally with a German works team. No process-intelligence or operations brand holds a Formula E partnership, so the lane is open.

Why Now

Celonis is in an active reposition-and-scale moment - the Context Model launch and Ikigai acquisition (May 2026) plus persistent IPO readiness mean brand attention and budget authority are elevated now. The enterprise/operations lane on the FE grid is uncontested, so moving before a peer notices it secures category ownership cheaply while the efficiency-and-sustainability narrative is freshest.

Why Porsche

Porsche, the German works team, is the natural cultural fit for a German enterprise champion, with a premium, industrial-tech-literate partner audience (AVEVA, TDK, NetApp) that mirrors Celonis's enterprise buyer. Celonis's process-intelligence lane is distinct from Porsche's AVEVA industrial digital-twin partnership, so it adds rather than conflicts - though the team is enterprise-software-adjacent, so Nissan (open 'enterprise software') is the documented cleaner-whitespace alternative.

Value to Porsche  — MODE B (tech serves the team operation)

MODE B. Celonis's platform maps a team-and-manufacturer's real operational processes - procurement, supply chain, logistics, manufacturing flow - from event-log data and surfaces where to cut cost, delay and waste. For a works operation like Porsche that is a genuine efficiency tool, not signage: the team gains operational insight and a credible 'engineered efficiency' co-marketing story, while Celonis gains a flagship European deployment and a sustainability-aligned brand stage.

Deal Architecture

Official Process Intelligence Partner tier on Porsche Formula E (Nissan as alternative), minimum three years, ~$3-5M/yr, with category exclusivity for process mining/operations intelligence, a working deployment on a defined operational workstream, and B2B hospitality aimed at Celonis's enterprise buyers.

Risks & Counters

No dated trigger  IPO-readiness without a fixed listing date means urgency can drift. Counter: Use category exclusivity as the clock - the open operations lane disappears the moment a peer signs - and anchor to the post-Ikigai brand momentum.
Enterprise-software adjacency at Porsche  Porsche already carries AVEVA (industrial software); a second enterprise-software-adjacent brand could feel crowded. Counter: Frame Celonis's process-intelligence lane as distinct from AVEVA's industrial digital twin; if the team prefers clean whitespace, re-point to Nissan ('enterprise software' open).
