The Case

Divergent Technologies, the AI-driven additive/digital-manufacturing leader, raised a $290M Series E at a $2.3B valuation (Sep 2025; $828M raised) and runs DAPS - an end-to-end platform that uses AI to design and 3D-print optimised, lightweight structural components and assemble them. The workstream is real and proven: Divergent already manufactures structural parts (front upper/lower wishbones, uprights) for McLaren Automotive's W1 hypercar - the same class of lightweight, performance-critical structures an F1 car needs. Crucially for the grid-check, that relationship is with McLaren AUTOMOTIVE (the road-car company), NOT the McLaren F1 team - they are separate businesses - so Divergent is grid-clean for an F1 sponsorship, and the McLaren brand-family connection is a warm bridge rather than a conflict. On-profile (advanced-manufacturing deep-tech). CEO Lukas Czinger (co-founder; with founder/chairman Kevin Czinger, who also built the Czinger 21C hypercar).

Why Now

Under the cost cap, the teams that iterate parts fastest and lightest win, and AI additive manufacturing is the frontier of that - Divergent is scaling DAPS now (post-Series E) and is investing in brand across automotive/aerospace/defense. The McLaren Automotive (W1) work is live proof, and the McLaren-family bridge is open today - engage before a rival manufacturing-tech brand claims the lane.

Why McLaren

McLaren Racing pairs a technology-forward, precision-engineering identity with a genuine warm path: Divergent already builds structural components for McLaren Automotive's W1, so the capability is proven within the McLaren family (noting Racing and Automotive are separate companies). The additive-manufacturing lane on the F1 team is open, and the value is a real co-engineering workstream on the car, not a logo.

Value to McLaren  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A with real, proven mechanics. (1) TECHNICAL (the core): F1 performance under the cost cap is decided by how fast a team designs, prints and iterates lightweight, stiffness-optimised structures - Divergent's DAPS does exactly that (AI topology optimisation + metal additive + automated assembly), and it is already producing this class of part (suspension wishbones, uprights) for the McLaren W1, so the transfer to F1 components is direct and deployable, not theoretical. (2) COMMERCIAL / BRAND: an American AI-manufacturing innovator on McLaren is a cutting-edge engineering story, and Divergent's aerospace/defense/auto customer network is a useful ecosystem; hospitality reaches advanced-manufacturing and OEM buyers. (3) TALENT: a recruiting halo for the additive/computational-design engineers both sides compete for. McLaren Racing gets a real, proven additive-manufacturing workstream and a frontier-tech partner; Divergent gets a flagship motorsport reference for DAPS.

Deal Architecture

Official Additive / Advanced-Manufacturing Partner on McLaren Racing, minimum three years, ~$3-5M/yr, with category exclusivity in additive/digital manufacturing and a co-engineering workstream printing structural components for the car (leveraging the proven McLaren W1 capability).

Risks & Counters

McLaren Automotive vs Racing distinction  The existing tie is with McLaren Automotive (road cars), a separate company from McLaren Racing (F1). Counter: Use it honestly as a warm brand-family bridge and proof the workstream works - not as an existing F1 deal; the F1 sponsorship would be net-new (grid-clean).
Value-tier capacity  At $2.3B, Divergent funds a value-to-mid deal, not a marquee spend. Counter: Right-size to a value-to-mid Official Additive-Manufacturing Partner tier tied to a real co-engineering component workstream.
Teams do additive in-house  F1 teams run their own AM facilities. Counter: DAPS is AI-design + metal AM + automated assembly at production quality (proven on the W1) - a step beyond typical in-house prototyping AM; position as a co-engineering capability upgrade, not a printer.
