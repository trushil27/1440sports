The Case

DSV, the Danish logistics group, completed its ~EUR14.3B acquisition of DB Schenker (integrating through 2028) to become the world's #1-2 freight forwarder - ~EUR41.6B revenue, ~160,000 people, 90+ countries. It surfaced on the MD's 8-Jul Prospect Radar, in the logistics whitespace that opens up now that AI and fintech categories are effectively sold out across the grid. The operational fit is genuine, not a halo: motorsport is fundamentally a global freight operation (logistics is the single biggest source of the sport's carbon footprint per F1's own reporting), and freight forwarders already sit at team level (DP World at McLaren, CEVA at Ferrari; DHL holds the F1 series deal). DSV's only motorsport tie so far is regional - Official Logistics Partner of Porsche Motorsport North America (Feb 2026), a Carrera Cup sportscar deal - so it is clean for an F1 team and that deal proves appetite to step up. HONEST framing: this is an operational/off-car partnership (freight + sustainable-freight decarbonisation), not in-car tech, and the Schenker integration is a multi-year effort - scored accordingly. Verified decision-maker: Group CEO Jens H. Lund (confirm the group marketing/sponsorship lead before outreach).

Why Now

DSV has just become the world's largest forwarder (Schenker close), which is exactly the identity moment when a company invests in a global brand platform, and it has just proven motorsport appetite (Porsche NA). Cadillac's clean-sheet team-build and open inventory (title + operational slots) make now the window to claim the logistics position before a rival forwarder does. The honest why-now is inflection- and land-grab-driven, not a dated race trigger.

Why Cadillac

Cadillac is the new, US-owned works team building its global race-and-factory logistics from zero, so a freight forwarder is a foundational operational partner (a genuine prime lane), and its US base aligns with DSV's US enterprise-growth pipeline and the Miami/Austin/Vegas hospitality DSV wants. No freight partner to conflict with (DP World=McLaren and CEVA=Ferrari are the taken team slots; DHL is series-level). It is a signal, not a placement - Cadillac's greenfield build is simply the best logistics fit (Williams and Aston Martin are the strong alternates, both with open freight lanes).

Value to Cadillac  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

A TWO-WAY exchange (business both directions), not a logo fee - three concrete swaps. (1) GLOBAL FREIGHT <-> MARQUEE STAGE: DSV runs the team's end-to-end global race-and-factory logistics (the integrated DP World-McLaren model) and de-risks the most logistically complex sport for a new team scaling worldwide; in return the team gives DSV the biggest global B2B stage and the 'world's largest forwarder, on the grid' brand moment against DHL. (2) SUSTAINABLE-FREIGHT DECARBONISATION <-> ESG STORY: DSV's biofuel fleet and carbon-optimised freight cut the team's single biggest carbon category (logistics) with auditable data for FIA / Net-Zero reporting; the team gives DSV a visible sustainability-leadership platform. (3) US ENTERPRISE PIPELINE <-> CLIENT ECOSYSTEM: the team's US races and hospitality open DSV's US enterprise-growth pipeline, while DSV's 90-country automotive/industrial client network becomes a B2B ecosystem the team can activate. Cadillac gets a foundational logistics operation + a decarbonisation story; DSV gets a global stage, US growth and a dethrone-DHL narrative.

Deal Architecture

Official Logistics / Freight Partner on Cadillac, minimum three years, multi-million (DSV is deeply deal-capable), structured as an integrated race-and-factory freight workstream + a sustainable-freight (biofuel fleet) decarbonisation programme with reported carbon data - value exchanged both ways, not a pure livery fee. A title-level conversation is credible given Cadillac's open ~$30-40M title ask.
