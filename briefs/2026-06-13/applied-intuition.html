The Case

Applied Intuition is the leader in 'physical AI' - vehicle software, simulation, validation and autonomy stacks - and in June 2025 it raised a $600M Series F at a $15B valuation (co-led by BlackRock-managed funds and Kleiner Perkins, $1.2B raised to date), while roughly doubling ARR to ~$830M. Its customers are 18 of the top 20 automakers (Toyota, VW, GM, Porsche, Stellantis among them), so the F1 paddock - dense with OEM and mobility decision-makers - is precisely the room it wants to be in ahead of a likely listing. Founder-led by Qasar Younis (ex-Y Combinator COO; early-career GM engineer), it has never touched motorsport. Crucially, the 'physical AI / autonomy' lane is open: the AI partnerships already on the grid are generic-enterprise (Anthropic as Williams' 'thinking partner', TWG as Cadillac's exclusive AI partner), not autonomous-vehicle software - a distinct category a single brand could own.

Why Now

Pre-IPO leaders invest in enterprise brand to support a future listing, and Applied Intuition's buyers - 18 of the top 20 automakers - cluster in the F1 paddock, so the audience ROI is unusually direct. The 'physical AI / autonomy' lane is unclaimed today, which rewards a first mover before the generic-AI deals expand to crowd it. There is no hard competitor clock yet, so the work is to create the urgency with an early, relationship-led approach to a founder-CEO who still owns brand strategy directly.

Why Haas

Haas is the lean, accessible entry tier with genuinely open enterprise-technology whitespace (its roster is largely consumer/commercial brands), and as the grid's American team it aligns with a Silicon Valley physical-AI leader's identity - a sensible, ownable first motorsport step at a sensible price, with a clear scale-up path to a larger team at the next contract if the model proves out.

Value to Haas  — MODE B (tech serves the team operation)

MODE B done with real substance - three concrete fronts. (1) COMMERCIAL / B2B ENGINE: Applied Intuition sells to 18 of the top 20 automakers, and the F1 paddock is wall-to-wall with exactly those OEM and mobility decision-makers - so Haas hospitality becomes a working enterprise pipeline channel for Applied Intuition, while Haas gains a premium, well-capitalized ($15B) American technology anchor partner whose presence signals engineering credibility and helps the team attract further blue-chip sponsors. Owning the unclaimed 'physical AI / autonomy' grid category is a defensible, single-brand position. (2) ENGINEERING ADJACENCY & TALENT: vehicle dynamics, simulation and controls are the shared language of any F1 engineering org and of Applied Intuition's product - a credible basis for co-developed 'simulation-driven development' content, advisory access to Applied Intuition's vehicle-software expertise, and a joint engineering-talent pipeline (both compete for the same ML/controls/vehicle-dynamics engineers); this is positioned as adjacency and knowledge-halo, explicitly NOT a claim that Haas runs Applied Intuition's simulator. (3) BRAND & CONTENT: an American team plus an American physical-AI champion is an aligned innovation narrative - a co-marketing content series on autonomy and physical AI, and a recruiting halo that lifts both brands with engineering talent. Haas gets a high-credibility, deep-pocketed partner and aligned co-marketing; Applied Intuition gets a premium B2B stage in front of its exact buyers ahead of an IPO.

Deal Architecture

Official Technology / Innovation Partner tier on Haas, minimum three years, ~$3-5M/yr, with category exclusivity in 'physical AI / autonomous-vehicle software' and a co-marketing + hospitality program built around the OEM-buyer audience.

Risks & Counters

Does a B2B infra company belong in F1?  Applied Intuition sells to engineers, not consumers, so the brand logic isn't obvious at first glance. Counter: The grid is now full of exactly this play - Atlassian, Anthropic, VAST Data, Keeper Security all sponsor for B2B reach; the F1 paddock IS Applied Intuition's buyer base, making the audience ROI unusually direct.
Engineering fit is adjacency, not in-car  Applied Intuition is not an F1 race-simulation vendor; overclaiming a technical deployment would be a defect. Counter: Position honestly as brand + B2B audience + engineering/talent adjacency; the value stands on audience match and category ownership alone.
Pre-IPO spend discipline  Public-company preparation favours visible efficiency over discretionary spend. Counter: Lead with a value-to-mid tier and a measurable OEM-pipeline outcome, not title-level spend.
