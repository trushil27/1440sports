The Case

Coralogix, a full-stack observability platform (logs, metrics, traces, APM and cloud-security/SIEM), closed a $200M Series F at a $1.6B valuation on 3 June 2026 - led by Advent and CPPIB, $550M raised to date - while operating at a $150-200M ARR run-rate (~60% growth) and serving 5,000+ customers including IBM, Tradeweb and JFrog. That gives it fresh capital and the brand-building motive of a scaling pre-IPO leader, and it has never entered motorsport. Observability is an open, ownable lane on the grid: Williams' enterprise roster is storage (VAST Data), collaboration (Atlassian) and security (Keeper) - no monitoring brand - while a direct rival, Splunk (now part of Cisco), already sits at McLaren. Co-founded and led by CEO Ariel Assaraf, Coralogix has a sitting CMO, Brian Mullen, who owns brand, partner ecosystem and strategic alliances - the precise, warm entry point.

Why Now

The Series F closed eleven days ago, so the capital and the brand mandate are live right now, and post-raise is exactly when a scaling platform invests in enterprise brand. There is also a competitor clock: Splunk-via-Cisco is already on the grid at McLaren, so claiming the open observability position - on a data-led team like Williams - is a move that both builds brand and denies the category to a rival. The decision sits with a sitting CMO (Brian Mullen) who owns alliances, so there is a clear, fast path to a yes.

Why Williams

Williams is a data-led 'engineering revival' team and a self-described preferred testing ground for enterprise software, with partners in storage, collaboration and security but none in observability - a distinct, unclaimed lane Coralogix could own. It is a credible, ownable stage (not title-level spend) for a $1.6B mid-cap, with a real deployment story across the team's software estate and a scale-up path if the model proves out.

Value to Williams  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A with real substance on three fronts. (1) TECHNICAL / OPS: Williams runs a sprawling, mission-critical software estate - race-weekend telemetry pipelines, factory CFD/simulation systems, trackside-to-factory data links and a growing enterprise-app stack - that has to be monitored in real time; Coralogix's full-stack observability (logs, metrics, traces, APM plus SIEM) is a genuine, deployable workstream to cut mean-time-to-resolution and catch anomalies before they cost track time, sitting naturally on top of VAST's storage layer and alongside Atlassian's engineering workflow. That is a flagship 'we monitor the team that races' reference deployment, not a logo. (2) COMMERCIAL / B2B: Williams' paddock audience is dense with the CIO/CTO/SRE and platform-engineering buyers Coralogix sells to, so hospitality becomes a working enterprise-pipeline channel, while Williams gains a well-capitalised ($1.6B) observability anchor that fits its proven enterprise-software-partner pattern and helps it keep attracting blue-chip tech deals - and owning the open observability category denies it to the Splunk-via-Cisco rival at McLaren. (3) BRAND / CONTENT / TALENT: a race weekend is the ultimate real-time, low-MTTR, anomaly-detection environment - a perfect, ownable analogy for Coralogix's value prop - powering a co-developed 'observability under pressure' content series, plus an SRE/platform-engineering recruiting halo both sides compete for. Williams gets a real monitoring deployment and aligned co-marketing; Coralogix gets a live reference and a premium B2B stage post-raise.

Deal Architecture

Official Observability Partner tier on Williams, minimum three years, ~$3-5M/yr, with category exclusivity in observability/APM and a working deployment monitoring the team's software estate.
