## THE CASE
Electra, founded in 2021, is France's leading urban EV fast-charging network, scaling toward 2,200 stations and 15,000 charge points across nine European countries by 2030. It has secured over €1B in total capital - a €304M Series B led by Dutch pension fund PGGM plus €433M of debt from a syndicate including ING and MUFG - is backed by EDF and SNCF's investment arm, and is, in its CEO's words, approaching unicorn status. It has never touched motorsport. EV charging is the single most on-message category for an all-electric championship, and Formula E is the rare property where the audience and the customer base are the same people: FE fans are EV-positive, urban, affluent European consumers - precisely the drivers Electra needs to choose its hubs. One honest nuance: ABB is FE's series-level charging partner (it charges the race cars), a distinct technical role from Electra's public-network brand, so this is a team-level brand-and-audience play, not a race-car-charging claim. Led by co-founder and CEO Aurélien de Meaux, the right entry point.

## WHY NOW
Electra is in an aggressive European land-grab - new markets, new cities, a race for driver mindshare against other charging networks - which is exactly when consumer-brand investment pays off, and Formula E reaches its target EV-driver audience in the very European cities it is entering. There is no hard clock, so the value of moving now is positional: lock a differentiated, on-message platform and the founder relationship while inbound interest is low (~30) and before a charging rival claims the space.
