The Case

Cohere is an enterprise-focused AI company (co-headquartered in Toronto and San Francisco) that has reached roughly $240M in ARR - beating its own $200M target - at a valuation of about $7B following a $500M August 2025 round (struck at $6.8B and since extended). It has just built out an IPO-grade leadership bench: a first CFO in ex-Uber finance chief Francois Chadwick, a Chief AI Officer in ex-Meta research head Joelle Pineau, and a CEO, Aidan Gomez, who has said publicly that a listing is coming 'soon'. Unlike consumer-AI labs, Cohere's whole positioning is enterprise-grade, secure, deployable 'sovereign AI you can trust in production' - a register that fits Formula E's enterprise-and-sustainability audience far better than a saturated F1 fintech lane. No frontier or enterprise-AI brand currently holds a Formula E team partnership, so the category is open, and an FE entry would give Cohere a differentiated, internationally-credible brand stage as it builds toward the public markets.

Why Now

With a first CFO installed and the CEO publicly guiding to an IPO 'soon', Cohere is squarely in the pre-listing brand-building window - when enterprise credibility is most valuable - and the enterprise-AI category in Formula E is empty. Moving now rewards a first mover before peers (and the larger AI labs) recognise the same open lane, while category exclusivity is uncontested and inexpensive.

Why Nissan Formula E

Nissan is the fit: a global EV pioneer with deep Formula E pedigree and an international, technology-forward brand that pairs naturally with Cohere's Canadian-and-Californian, enterprise-AI identity. The team's electric-mobility story and Cohere's 'practical AI for real enterprises' story reinforce each other, and Nissan's audience skews to exactly the kind of large, regulated enterprises Cohere sells to.

Value to Nissan Formula E  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A. AI-driven race strategy, energy-management modelling for an all-electric car, and large-scale data analysis are genuine Formula E problems where Cohere's enterprise models could be deployed as a working technical partnership, not just branding. Nissan gains a real AI collaboration and a credible innovation narrative; Cohere gains a live, demonstrable deployment it can reference with enterprise buyers and investors.

Deal Architecture

Official Enterprise AI Partner tier on Nissan Formula E, minimum three years, ~$3-5M/yr, with AI-category exclusivity and a defined technical workstream (strategy/energy modelling) so the partnership has substance.

Primary Decision-Maker

Aidan Gomez
Co-Founder & CEO, Cohere - verified (no CMO; the President/COO seat was vacated when Martin Kon moved to an advisory/board role in 2025)
Cohere is founder-led and Gomez is closely involved in brand and strategic-partnership decisions, so the CEO is the correct entry point - there is no CMO, and former President/COO Martin Kon stepped into an advisory role in 2025. Co-decision-maker on spend: Francois Chadwick, hired in 2025 as Cohere's first CFO (ex-Uber), who owns the IPO-readiness budget. A MODE-A technical workstream would also engage Chief AI Officer Joelle Pineau (ex-Meta research head).

Opening Angle

"Aidan, Cohere's whole edge is enterprise-grade, deployable AI - Formula E is the enterprise-and-sustainability grid, and no AI brand owns it yet. Nissan has no AI partner and a real strategy/energy workstream waiting - not a logo. 25 minutes before a rival lab notices?"
