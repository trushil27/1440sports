The Case

Bloom Energy (NYSE: BE) is the breakout winner of the AI-driven 'bring-your-own-power' crunch: Q1'26 revenue rose 130% to $751M (product revenue +208%), FY26 guidance is $3.4-3.8B, backlog sits near $20B, and the stock is up 219% YTD on data-centre power deals with Brookfield ($5B), Oracle and a 328MW order from Nebius. Its solid-oxide fuel cells deliver clean-er, resilient on-site power where the grid can't keep up. It has never entered motorsport. On-profile (power/energy) and a real fit: a modern F1 operation - factory, wind tunnel, an energy-hungry CFD/compute data centre and trackside - needs resilient power, and on-site generation is exactly Bloom's domain. HONEST framing: fuel cells run on (lower-carbon, hydrogen-capable) natural gas, so this suits F1's hybrid/sustainable-fuel era rather than all-electric FE; and 'facility power' overlaps the Vertiv/Eaton theme, so brand_fit is scored down. Founder-CEO KR Sridhar.

Why Now

Bloom is at the crest of the AI-power demand cycle (record growth, a ~$20B backlog, a stock up 219% YTD), so capital and brand visibility are peaking, and F1's energy-intensive 2026 era makes resilient on-site power on-message. The Nebius 328MW deal shows the 'power-behind-the-compute' thesis is live - move while the category is hot.

Why Williams

Williams is a data-and-compute-led team (VAST, Anthropic) with energy-intensive simulation and an open on-site-power lane, so 'the power behind the compute that designs the car' is an on-narrative fit. The value is resilient, efficient on-site generation for the team's facilities and compute, not car technology.

Value to Williams  — MODE A (tech in the car / championship)

MODE A with real mechanics. (1) TECHNICAL (the core): an F1 team's factory, wind tunnel and (increasingly) its CFD/AI data centre are energy-intensive and cannot lose power - Bloom's on-site solid-oxide generation supplies resilient, efficient, lower-carbon power independent of grid constraints, a genuine, deployable workstream that gets more valuable as compute loads climb under the cost cap. (2) COMMERCIAL / B2B: Bloom sells to hyperscalers, manufacturers and data-centre operators who populate the F1 paddock (and it powers Nebius), so hospitality becomes a working channel, while Williams gains a fast-scaling energy anchor. (3) BRAND / TALENT: 'the power behind performance' ties Bloom to the AI-power era on a data-driven sport, with an energy/engineering talent halo. Williams gets resilient on-site power and a blue-chip anchor; Bloom gets a visible reference at its peak.

Deal Architecture

Official On-Site Power Partner on Williams, minimum three years, ~$4-6M/yr, with category exclusivity in on-site power generation and a deployment powering the team's facilities, data centre and trackside operations.

Risks & Counters

Gas optic for motorsport  Solid-oxide fuel cells run on natural gas, which sits awkwardly against a sustainability-forward sport. Counter: Position for F1's hybrid/sustainable-fuel era (not all-electric FE), emphasise lower-carbon, resilient, hydrogen-capable on-site power and measurable efficiency - not zero-carbon claims.
Overlaps the facility-power theme  Power-for-facilities is adjacent to Vertiv (power+cooling) and Eaton (electrical). Counter: Bloom's lane is on-site GENERATION (vs distribution/cooling) - a distinct category; position precisely and on a different team (Williams).
Large-cap, many priorities  At peak demand Bloom may not prioritise one team. Counter: Frame as a flagship 'power-behind-the-compute' reference (it already powers Nebius) and a B2B-pipeline channel, right-sized to a real deployment.
